Rules explained

Volleyball timeouts and technical timeouts

Two kinds of stoppage look identical on the scoreboard but work nothing alike. One is requested by a coach or captain. The other fires on its own the moment a team's score crosses a number nobody chose.

Regular timeouts

A regular timeout is requested by a coach or captain, with the ball out of play. Most competitions allow two per team per set, lasting around 30 seconds each, though both the count and the length vary by competition.

Technical timeouts: automatic, not requested

A technical timeout is not requested by anyone — it triggers automatically the moment the leading team's score reaches a set threshold in a regular set.

The rule that matters

Technical timeouts fire automatically when the leading team reaches 8 and again at 16 points in a regular set, typically lasting 60 seconds. They are common in higher-level competition and often switched off entirely in domestic and local play.

The deciding set drops technical timeouts

The deciding set — the last possible set of the match — normally has no technical timeouts at all, even in competitions that use them in every earlier set. Regular, coach-requested timeouts can still be available in the deciding set; it is only the automatic 8-and-16 stoppages that drop out.

The interval between sets

Between sets there is a separate break, the set interval, typically around three minutes. It is distinct from a mid-set timeout: it happens automatically after every set, including before the deciding set, and it is when teams switch ends of the court for the next set.

A timeout never rotates anybody

Calling a timeout does not change anyone's position on court. Play resumes exactly as it was before the stoppage — same server, same rotation, same lineup. Rotation only happens on a side-out, never on a timeout.

Common questions

What is a technical timeout in volleyball?

A technical timeout is an automatic break that is not requested by either team. It is triggered the moment the leading team's score reaches 8 or 16 points in a regular set, and typically lasts 60 seconds. Many domestic and local competitions switch technical timeouts off entirely.

How many regular timeouts does each team get per set?

Two per team per set is the most common allowance, at 30 seconds each, but this is one of the rules that varies the most by competition. SpikeSheet lets you set both the number and the duration to match whatever rules your match is being played under.

Are there technical timeouts in the deciding set?

Normally, no. The deciding set typically drops technical timeouts entirely, even in competitions that use them in every other set.
