Rules explained

How to keep score in volleyball

Every rally scores a point, sets go to 25, and you have to win by two clear points. The part that trips up new scorers is the last set of the match — it only goes to 15, and it comes with a side switch nobody remembers until it happens.

Rally scoring: every rally scores

Volleyball uses rally point scoring. Whichever team wins a rally gets a point, full stop — it does not matter which team served, and it does not matter how the rally was won. Older scoring systems only awarded a point to the serving team and made everyone else wait for the serve back before they could score. Rally scoring replaced that everywhere, so a team can win points while receiving serve just as easily as while serving it.

The only thing serving still decides is whether winning the rally also hands your team the next serve. If you were already serving and you win the rally, you keep serving. If you win the rally while receiving, you take the serve away from the other team — a side-out.

Winning a set: 25 points, two clear

A regular set is won by the first team to reach 25 points, provided they are at least two points ahead. Reaching 25 first is not enough on its own if the other team is right behind you.

The rule that matters

A set cannot end on a one-point lead. At 24-24, the set keeps going — 25-24 is not a win, 26-25 is not a win, and play continues indefinitely until someone is two points clear. This is called deuce, and there is no cap on how long it can run.

Winning the match: best of three or best of five

A match is won by whichever team wins the majority of the sets. Most competitions play best-of-five, where the first team to three sets wins the match. Some competitions play best-of-three instead, where two sets is enough. Check which format your competition uses before the match starts, since it changes how many sets you might need to track and, more importantly, which set is the deciding one.

The deciding set: 15 points, and a side switch at 8

The last possible set of the match — the third in a best-of-three, the fifth in a best-of-five — is the deciding set, and it is played to a lower target of 15 points instead of 25. The two-clear-points rule still applies in full: 15-14 is not a win, and the set continues past that just like a regular set continues past 25.

The deciding set also has one feature the earlier sets do not: when either team reaches 8 points, the two teams switch ends of the court. Play carries straight on from the score it was at — nothing about the score or the serve changes, only which end of the court each team is defending.

What a scorer actually writes down

After every rally, a scorer records two things: which team won the rally, and the running score for both teams that results from it. Whenever the rally causes a side-out — the receiving team wins the point and takes over serve — that is worth marking distinctly, since it is also the moment the new server's team rotates. Between sets, note the final score, then start a fresh column or sheet for the next set rather than carrying the score forward.

The order matters more than it sounds: record the point first, then check whether it was a side-out, then update serve and rotation. Doing it the other way around is how a sheet ends up with the wrong team credited for a point it did not actually win.

Common questions

How many sets are in a volleyball match?

It depends on the competition format. Most matches are best-of-five, so the first team to win three sets wins the match. Some competitions play best-of-three instead, where two sets is enough. Either way, the last possible set is the deciding set and is played to 15 rather than 25.

What happens at 24-24 in volleyball?

Nothing ends the set yet. A set has to be won by two clear points, so at 24-24 play continues past 25 until one team gets two points ahead, whether that happens at 26-24, 27-25, or later. This is the same rule that applies at 14-14 in the 15-point deciding set.

Does it matter who was serving when a point is scored?

No. Volleyball uses rally point scoring, so every rally ends in a point for whichever team wins it, regardless of which team served. The only thing serving decides is whether winning the rally also wins your team the next serve.
